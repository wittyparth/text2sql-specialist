## Chinook set details
- Difficulty: easy 25 · medium 35 · hard 25 · sqlite_specific 15. All 11 tables are covered (Track 43 items … MediaType 5).
- Validator (`data/eval/validate_chinook_eval.py`):
  - each item is read-only, executes, is non-empty and returns `expected_row_count` rows
  - the `tables` field matches the SQL
  - determinism: results don't change under extra ascending/descending sort keys
  - no ties at top-N cutoffs; ties inside ordered results produce a warning (3 warnings, each reviewed: the question states its tie-break)
- **Human review step (T3.0, 15 min):** read 20 random items and fix any wording ambiguity, then freeze. Already-flagged strictness traps (086 year type, 055 datetime format, 098 float) are handled by the metric normalization.
- Provenance is stated in the README: "generated by an LLM agent, every item validated by execution, then spot-checked by hand". That's honest and standard practice.

## Why two sets
The Gretel held-out set shows the fine-tune **worked** on the target distribution. Chinook shows whether it **transfers** to a realistic database. If fine-tuning helps on Gretel but hurts on Chinook, that's an important, reportable finding: overfitting to synthetic templates. The distractor augmentation (stage 08) is designed to prevent it.
